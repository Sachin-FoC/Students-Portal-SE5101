Divisibility & GCDThe basic words we use everywhere

Divides

a | b

“a divides b” means b = a × k for some whole number k.
Example: 3 | 12 because 12 = 3 × 4
GCD

gcd(a, b)

The largest number that divides both a and b.
Example: gcd(18, 30) = 6
Coprime

gcd(a, b) = 1

a and b share no common factor except 1.
Example: gcd(17, 23) = 1 → coprime
“Coprime” (also called relatively prime) is the most important word in this lecture. Almost every rule below starts with “if gcd(a, n) = 1…”.
2

The Euclidean AlgorithmA fast way to find the GCD, without factoring

gcd(a, b) = gcd(b, a mod b)
Divide the big number by the small one and keep the remainder. Swap, and repeat. When the remainder becomes 0, the last non-zero remainder is the GCD.

Example: gcd(252, 105)

  1. 252 = 105 × 2 + 42
  2. 105 = 42 × 2 + 21
  3. 42 = 21 × 2 + 0  ← remainder 0, stop
  4. gcd(252, 105) = 21

Why it matters: factoring huge numbers is very slow, but Euclid's method is fast even for numbers with hundreds of digits.

Modular Arithmetic“Clock maths”: only the remainder matters

a mod n

The remainder when a is divided by n.
17 mod 5 = 2  (17 = 3×5 + 2)

a ≡ b (mod n)

“a is congruent to b” = they give the same remainder when divided by n.
17 ≡ 2 (mod 5)
Think of a clock with n hours. After reaching n−1, you go back to 0. On a 12-hour clock, 15 o'clock is the same as 3, so 15 ≡ 3 (mod 12).
You can take “mod n” at any step and the final answer stays the same. That keeps numbers small in RSA.
4

Additive & Multiplicative InversesHow to “undo” + and × under mod

Additive inverse
(a + b) mod n = 0
The answer is simply b = n − a.
Inverse of 4 mod 10 is 6, since 4 + 6 = 10 ≡ 0
Always exists.
Multiplicative inverse
(a × x) mod n = 1
Written a−1.
Exists only if gcd(a, n) = 1.
4 has no inverse mod 10 (gcd = 2)

Example: find the inverse of 3 mod 7

Just try x = 1, 2, 3, … until (3 × x) mod 7 = 1:

3−1 ≡ 5 (mod 7)

Trying every number works for small n. For the huge numbers in RSA, we use the Extended Euclidean Algorithm, which finds the inverse quickly.

Euler's Totient Function φ(n)“How many numbers below n are coprime with n?”

φ(n) counts how many numbers from 1 up to n have no common factor with n (except 1).
Example: φ(5) = 4, because 1, 2, 3, 4 are all coprime with 5.
Example: φ(10) = 4, because only 1, 3, 7, 9 are coprime with 10.

Three shortcut formulas

n is prime p
φ(p) = p − 1
φ(7) = 6
n = p × q (two primes)
φ(n) = (p−1)(q−1)
φ(15) = 2 × 4 = 8  (used in RSA)
n = pk (prime power)
φ(pk) = pk − pk−1
φ(9) = 9 − 3 = 6

Fermat's Little TheoremA shortcut for powers when the modulus is prime

If p is prime and gcd(a, p) = 1 (a is not divisible by p), then:
ap−1 ≡ 1 (mod p)   i.e.   ap−1 mod p = 1
Raise a to the power p − 1, divide by p, and the remainder is always 1.

Example: p = 7, a = 3

  1. 7 is prime ✓ and gcd(3, 7) = 1 ✓
  2. 37−1 = 36 = 729
  3. 729 mod 7 = 1  (729 = 104 × 7 + 1)
  4. 36 ≡ 1 (mod 7)
7

Euler's TheoremFermat's idea for any n (prime or not)

For any integer n > 1, if gcd(a, n) = 1, then:
aφ(n) ≡ 1 (mod n)   i.e.   aφ(n) mod n = 1
If a and n are coprime and you raise a to the power φ(n), the remainder when dividing by n is always 1.

Step by step: a = 3, n = 10

  1. Check coprime: gcd(3, 10) = 1 ✓ so the theorem applies
  2. Find φ(10): coprimes are 1, 3, 7, 9 → φ(10) = 4
  3. Raise a to φ(n): 34 = 81
  4. Take mod n: 81 mod 10 = 1
  5. 34 ≡ 1 (mod 10)

Practical example: 74 mod 10

φ(10) = 4 and gcd(7, 10) = 1, so Euler says the answer is 1.
Check: 74 = 2401, and 2401 mod 10 = 1 ✓

These properties make modular exponentiation (big powers under mod) practical in encryption.

Fermat vs Euler: how they connect

Fermat is just a special case of Euler. When n = p is prime, φ(p) = p − 1, so Euler's aφ(n) ≡ 1 becomes Fermat's ap−1 ≡ 1.

Why This Matters for RSAPutting all the pieces together

Key setup

1. Pick two primes p, q
2. n = p × q
3. φ(n) = (p−1)(q−1)
4. Pick e and d so that
e · d ≡ 1 (mod φ(n))
So d is the multiplicative inverse of e (needs gcd(e, φ(n)) = 1).

Encrypt / Decrypt

Encrypt: C = Me mod n
Decrypt: M = Cd mod n

Cd = (Me)d = Me·d mod n
Euler's theorem guarantees this gives back the original M.

These tools are also the basis of key generation, modular inverse computations and digital signature algorithms.

Practice QuestionsTry by hand first, then open the answer

Q1Find gcd(48, 18) using the Euclidean algorithm.
48 = 18 × 2 + 12 → 18 = 12 × 1 + 6 → 12 = 6 × 2 + 0.  gcd = 6
Q2Is 14 and 25 coprime?
14 = 2 × 7, 25 = 5 × 5. No common factor → gcd(14, 25) = 1. Yes, coprime.
Q3Find the additive and multiplicative inverse of 4 mod 9.
Additive: 9 − 4 = 5 (4 + 5 = 9 ≡ 0).
Multiplicative: gcd(4, 9) = 1 so it exists. 4 × 7 = 28 = 3 × 9 + 1 → 4−1 ≡ 7 (mod 9)
Q4Does 6 have a multiplicative inverse mod 9? Why?
No. gcd(6, 9) = 3 ≠ 1, so no inverse exists.
Q5Find φ(7), φ(15) and φ(9).
φ(7) = 7 − 1 = 6 (prime)
φ(15) = (3−1)(5−1) = 8 (15 = 3 × 5)
φ(9) = 9 − 3 = 6 (9 = 3²)
Q6Use Fermat's theorem to find 26 mod 7.
7 is prime and gcd(2, 7) = 1, so 27−1 ≡ 1. Answer: 1 (check: 64 = 9 × 7 + 1)
Q7Use Euler's theorem to find 34 mod 8.
φ(8) = 8 − 4 = 4 and gcd(3, 8) = 1, so 34 ≡ 1. Answer: 1 (check: 81 = 10 × 8 + 1)
Q8What is the last digit of 3100?
Last digit = value mod 10. φ(10) = 4 and 100 = 25 × 4, so 3100 = (34)25 ≡ 125 = 1. Last digit is 1.
Q9Mini RSA: p = 3, q = 11, e = 3. Find n, φ(n) and d.
n = 3 × 11 = 33
φ(n) = 2 × 10 = 20
Need 3 × d ≡ 1 (mod 20): 3 × 7 = 21 ≡ 1 → d = 7
Bonus: encrypt M = 4 → C = 4³ mod 33 = 64 mod 33 = 31. Decrypt → 31⁷ mod 33 = 4 ✓ (check with the power widget above)

SummaryOne-line memory card for each topic

  • GCD: largest number dividing both. gcd = 1 means coprime.
  • Euclidean algorithm: gcd(a, b) = gcd(b, a mod b), repeat until remainder 0.
  • Modular arithmetic: a mod n is the remainder; a ≡ b (mod n) means same remainder.
  • Additive inverse: b = n − a. Multiplicative inverse: (a × x) mod n = 1, only when gcd(a, n) = 1.
  • Totient: φ(p) = p − 1  ·  φ(pq) = (p−1)(q−1)  ·  φ(pk) = pk − pk−1
  • Fermat: ap−1 ≡ 1 (mod p), p prime. Euler: aφ(n) ≡ 1 (mod n). Fermat is a special case of Euler.
  • RSA: n = pq, φ(n) = (p−1)(q−1), e·d ≡ 1 (mod φ(n)), C = Me mod n, M = Cd mod n.
