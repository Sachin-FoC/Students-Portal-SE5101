    Topic 01 · Basic Security Concepts

    Before any algorithm or protocol, security starts with a mindset: assume an intelligent adversary, not just accidents.

    Computer and network security is the practice of protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information and computing systems — from the data sitting on a disk to the packets travelling across the internet.

    Traditional software correctness asks "does this work under normal conditions?" Security asks a harder question: "does this still hold up when someone is actively trying to break it?" That shift — from accidental failure to adversarial failure — is what makes security its own discipline.

    It matters more every year because more of daily life runs over networks: banking, healthcare records, critical infrastructure, and private communication all depend on the guarantees this course will formalize.

    Adversarial thinking Confidentiality Integrity Availability Trust boundaries
    Topic 01 · Basic Security Concepts

    Nearly every security requirement traces back to one of these three goals — the CIA triad.

    C
    Confidentiality
    Information is disclosed only to those authorized to see it. Broken by eavesdropping or data leaks.
    I
    Integrity
    Information is modified only by authorized parties, in authorized ways. Broken by tampering.
    A
    Availability
    Authorized parties can access information and systems when needed. Broken by denial-of-service.
    Topic 01 · Basic Security Concepts

    The ITU-T X.800 framework breaks the CIA triad into concrete services a system can provide.

    Authentication
    Confirms the identity of a communicating party or the origin of data.
    Access Control
    Restricts what an authenticated party is permitted to do.
    Data Confidentiality
    Protects data from unauthorized disclosure, in transit or at rest.
    Data Integrity
    Detects any unauthorized modification of data.
    Non-repudiation
    Prevents a sender or receiver from denying a transmitted message.
    Availability
    Ensures authorized access to systems and data isn't disrupted.
    Topic 01 · Basic Security Concepts

    Attacks on a network fall into two broad classes, distinguished by whether the attacker alters the data stream.

    Passive Attacks — Observe

    • EavesdroppingReading message contents in transit without altering them.
    • Traffic AnalysisInferring information from communication patterns even when content is encrypted.

    Active Attacks — Alter

    • MasqueradeImpersonating another entity to gain unauthorized access.
    • ReplayCapturing and later re-sending legitimate data to produce an unauthorized effect.
    • ModificationAltering a legitimate message in transit.
    • Denial of ServicePreventing or degrading the normal use of a system or network.

    Passive attacks are hard to detect but can be prevented with encryption; active attacks are hard to prevent but can often be detected.
